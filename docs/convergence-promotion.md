# Convergence-Based Promotion

## What It Is

Convergence-based promotion is the mechanism that moves ideas from Literature Notes — records of what sources said — to Permanent Notes — records of what you believe, supported by evidence. It is the central act of knowledge synthesis. Everything before it is preparation; everything after it is application.

Most PKM systems treat this transition as informal: you read enough, you feel confident enough, you write a synthesis. The problem is that "confident enough" is not a reliable signal. You can feel confident after one persuasive book. You can feel perpetually uncertain after twenty books that never quite align. Informal synthesis leaves the decision to intuition, which is biased toward both premature confidence and indefinite procrastination.

Convergence-based promotion replaces that intuition with a test. The question is not "do I feel ready to synthesize?" but "have two or more independent sources made the same structural claim?" If yes, the test is passed. If no, the note stays in the queue. The threshold is explicit, and it doesn't move.

---

## The Convergence Test

Before promoting any cluster of Literature Notes into a Permanent Note, four questions must be answered affirmatively. All four.

**1. Independence: Do these notes come from sources that couldn't have directly influenced each other on this specific claim?**

Two books by the same author don't qualify. A book and its popularization don't qualify. Two sources that both trace their claim back to the same study or theorist don't qualify — they're amplifying one signal, not producing two independent ones. The independence requirement is strict because the entire epistemological value of convergence rests on it. If the sources influenced each other, their agreement tells you nothing beyond what one source already contained.

**2. Mechanism alignment: Do the sources agree on why the pattern holds, not just that it holds?**

Topical overlap is common. Mechanistic agreement is rare. Two sources can both write about the importance of feedback in learning while proposing completely different causal mechanisms — one arguing that feedback works because it corrects misconceptions, another arguing it works because it provides motivational cues. These sources are not converging on the same claim. They're both addressing feedback, but they're making different claims about how it operates. Promotion requires that the underlying explanation is compatible, not just the surface-level subject matter.

**3. Generalizability: Is the pattern recognizable outside the specific context where it was first described?**

A principle that only holds within one narrow domain is a domain observation, not a generalizable insight. This doesn't mean cross-domain generalization is required for every Permanent Note — but it does mean the claim needs to be articulable in terms that would be recognizable to someone from a different field. If you can only express the principle in the vocabulary of one discipline, you may be dealing with a domain-specific observation rather than a genuine pattern.

**4. Own-words test: Can you state the principle in your own words without quoting or paraphrasing either source?**

If you need the source's phrasing to express the idea, you haven't absorbed the idea — you've memorized the phrasing. The own-words test is not about changing vocabulary for its own sake. It's a proxy for actual understanding: if you can express the claim independently, you understand the structure of the claim. If you can only reproduce the source's expression, you may be promoting a paraphrase rather than a principle.

## The Promotion Process

Passing the convergence test tells you that promotion is warranted. The promotion itself is a five-step process.

### Step 1: Identify the candidate cluster

A cluster is a group of two or more Literature Notes, from different independent sources, that appear to be making the same structural claim. The cluster may be obvious — you noticed the pattern while reading — or it may require deliberate comparison. Either way, the starting point is a set of existing Literature Notes, not a blank page.

### Step 2: Run the convergence test

Apply all four questions to the cluster. If any question fails, the notes are not ready for promotion. The right response is to continue reading, looking for sources that strengthen the cluster — or to conclude that the convergence doesn't genuinely exist and that what appeared to be alignment is actually a coincidence of topic.

### Step 3: Draft the Permanent Note

A Permanent Note has a specific structure:

- **A title that is a falsifiable claim**: Not "thoughts on feedback" but "Feedback only improves performance when the recipient has the agency to act on it." A title you can disagree with.
- **A body that explains the mechanism in your own words**: Not a summary of what each source said, but your synthesis of what they jointly establish. The sources appear as evidence, not as the content.
- **Source citations**: The Literature Notes that provided the convergent evidence. Each cited source should have passed the independence test.
- **Boundary conditions**: Where does this claim break down? What conditions must be present for it to hold? What would falsify it? Boundary conditions are not optional — they are what distinguish a serious claim from an overgeneralization.

### Step 4: The challenge test

Before finalizing any Permanent Note, spend ten minutes arguing the opposing claim using evidence from your own vault. Not devil's advocate for its own sake — a genuine attempt to find counterexamples, contradicting sources, or limiting cases. If the note survives the challenge, record the best counterargument as an additional boundary condition. If it doesn't survive, the note belongs back in the candidate cluster at Rung 3, not in the Permanent Notes folder.

The challenge test is the single most important quality gate in the promotion process. Premature synthesis is the failure mode it guards against: the tendency to promote a note after one compelling source, before genuine convergence has been established. A note that fails the challenge test under your own scrutiny will fail it even faster when used in real work.

### Step 5: Assign provisional status

Every newly promoted Permanent Note begins provisional. Provisional does not mean weak — it means the note has passed the evidence test but not yet the use test. It represents your best current synthesis of what multiple independent sources agree on. It has not yet been tested in practice. Endorsement — the step that moves a note from provisional to trusted — comes later, through use.

## The Endorsement State Machine

Promoted notes exist in one of three states. The transitions between them are explicit and irreversible except by deliberate revision.

```
provisional → endorsed
provisional → rejected
```

There is no automatic progression. Time does not advance a provisional note to endorsed. Only use does.

**Provisional**: The note passed the convergence test and was written up as a Permanent Note. It has evidence but no use history. It should be retrieved and used, but with the awareness that it hasn't been tested in practice yet.

**Endorsed**: You used this note in actual output — a piece of writing, a decision, a presentation, a conversation with stakes attached — and it held up. The principle generalized. The claim was accurate. You would use it again. Endorsement is an explicit act: you mark the note as endorsed, and the reason is implicit in the use that produced it.

**Rejected**: You used this note in practice and it failed. Or your thinking has evolved past the position it expresses, and you no longer hold it. Or new evidence contradicts the convergence it was built on. Rejection is not deletion. The note moves to a rejected archive with one required addition: the reason for rejection.

The rejection reason is the most valuable field in the system. "Doesn't generalize past early-stage companies — the mechanism breaks when organizational complexity exceeds a threshold" tells you more than the original note did. It shows you where the principle's boundary conditions actually lie — not where you guessed they lay when you wrote it, but where you discovered them through use.

## Promotion Quality Tests

Beyond the primary convergence test, three secondary tests assess the quality of a promotion decision.

**The stranger test**: Could someone unfamiliar with either source read your Permanent Note and recognize the underlying pattern in a completely different domain? If the note can only be understood by someone who's read the same books you read, it may still be too source-dependent.

**The prediction test**: Does the permanent note generate a testable prediction? "Small structural advantages compound faster than most people expect" implies that in any domain you examine, early advantages should be disproportionately durable. A principle that makes no predictions is difficult to use — and difficult to falsify, which means you'll never know whether it was right.

**The connection test**: How many existing notes in your vault does the new Permanent Note link to, extend, or partially contradict? A note that connects to nothing may be expressing a genuinely isolated insight — or it may be using vocabulary that doesn't map onto how you've indexed related concepts. Either way, a note without connections is harder to retrieve and harder to compound with other knowledge.

## Why Endorsement State Matters at Retrieval Time

When you pull knowledge as context — for writing, for reasoning, for AI-assisted production — the quality of the output depends on the quality of the context you inject. Not all notes contribute equally.

An endorsed note is a position you've tested in practice and confirmed. You used it under real conditions, it worked, and you explicitly approved it. When you inject it as context, you're injecting a stable, tested belief.

A provisional note is a position with good evidence but no use history. It might be equally valuable — or it might be a synthesis that looked compelling in the library and fails in practice. When you inject it as context, you're injecting a hypothesis.

Treating endorsed and provisional notes as equivalent is like treating a hypothesis and a replicated result as equivalent — they're both part of the knowledge system, but they're not the same kind of evidence. Retrieving endorsed notes preferentially for output contexts produces better work, and it does so consistently rather than by accident.

---

## What This Is Not

**Not accumulation**: The number of Permanent Notes in your vault is not a health metric. A vault with 30 endorsed notes that you've tested and trust is more valuable than a vault with 300 provisional notes that you've never used. Promotion is a quality gate, not a volume operation.

**Not a one-time process**: A note you promoted two years ago based on the best evidence available then may need to be demoted if new evidence contradicts it, or if the context where it applies has changed. The endorsement state machine runs in both directions: endorsed notes can return to provisional if you discover their applicability is narrower than you thought. The archive preserves the history; the current state reflects your current assessment.

**Not confidence theater**: The convergence test exists to prevent a specific failure mode — promoting notes because you feel confident, rather than because the evidence warrants confidence. The test is not a bureaucratic formality. It is the mechanism by which the feeling of confidence becomes grounded in evidence rather than enthusiasm.

## Measuring Impact

| Metric | Formula | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Promotion ratio | Permanent Notes / Literature Notes | > 5% signals active synthesis |
| Endorsement rate | Endorsed PNs / Total PNs | > 30% for vaults > 6 months old |
| Rejection ratio | Rejected PNs / Total promoted over 12 months | 5–15% is healthy; near-zero suggests insufficient testing |
| Challenge survival rate | PNs that survived challenge test / Total PNs created | > 90%; below this, premature synthesis is occurring |
| Time to first endorsement | Avg. days from promotion to first endorsement | < 90 days; longer suggests notes aren't being used in output |
